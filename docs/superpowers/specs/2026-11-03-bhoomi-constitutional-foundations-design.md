## Purpose

Add the first coherent governance layer for The Bhoomi Federation: a central canon registry plus an original constitutional charter and concise government, legal-order and citizenship foundations. The slice must clearly distinguish newly created 2026 fictional canon from the five original 2025 records, keep the existing portal’s visual system, and make the legal and institutional relationships understandable without implying real-world statehood or operating services.

## Source-backed foundation

The existing primary record establishes the founding date and four values in BHF/003/2025; BHF/004/2025 establishes the demonym “Bhoomians” but leaves eligibility and a grant process unstated. These records remain unchanged and are linked as historical anchors, not rewritten as if they contained later provisions.

## Canon choices

### Geography and federalism

The Constitution describes a civic federation but establishes no territory, boundary, capital, population, member-unit name or unit count. Its member-unit schedule remains blank. The charter assigns national authority only over explicitly listed federation-wide responsibilities (the rights floor, citizenship, national elections and constitutional adjudication); local self-government is reserved to member units only after future canon lawfully defines them. Concurrent areas need legislation stating responsibility and coordination, and a federal law prevails only where the Constitution expressly grants that priority. Inter-unit disputes go to the Constitutional Court. This makes the federal relationship legible without inventing geography.

### Rights and limits

Rights attach to every person, not only citizens: dignity, equality, life, conscience, expression, assembly and association, privacy, movement, fair hearing, access to courts, an effective remedy and access to public information. Children’s identity and participation receive explicit protection. Rights limits require a published general law, a legitimate public purpose, necessity and proportionality, and preservation of the right’s core. In emergencies, life, dignity, freedom from torture and slavery, legal personality, protection from retroactive criminal punishment, conscience, court access and habeas review cannot be suspended. Core social guarantees are advanced through law and transparent budgets rather than unsupported numerical promises.

### Government and institutions

The People’s Assembly is elected every four years by proportional representation. Each future member unit sends one Council delegate, selected under its own published constitution for a four-year term; each unit has one equal vote. Unit names and the member-unit schedule remain undefined until later canon establishes them, so the Council is not operational in this slice. The two chambers pass federal laws and budgets.

A Federation Convenor is the ceremonial head of state, elected in a joint sitting for one nonrenewable five-year term and limited to constitutional functions. The Prime Minister is chosen by the People’s Assembly and remains in office only while it has confidence; a constructive no-confidence vote names a successor. The Cabinet is accountable to the Assembly. Ministries are created by statute and budget, not invented as a disconnected list now.

All courts are independent. Ordinary judges are selected through open, merit-based competition on Judicial Appointments Commission recommendation, formally appointed by the Convenor, and serve until resignation, a fixed retirement age of 70 or removal for proven incapacity or serious misconduct after independent tribunal review. The nine-member Constitutional Court has staggered, nonrenewable twelve-year terms; appointments from a public shortlist require a three-fifths vote in the Assembly and a majority in the Council, followed by formal appointment by the Convenor. A nine-member Judicial Appointments Commission has three judges elected by their peers, two members chosen by the legal profession, two public members elected by the Assembly (one from the governing majority and one from the opposition), and two unit delegates chosen by the Council. Commission members serve one nonrenewable five-year term; the Commission remains non-operational until the Council exists. Removal of Constitutional Court judges is limited to proven incapacity or serious misconduct after independent tribunal review and a two-thirds vote in both chambers.

Three independent institutions serve distinct purposes: an Electoral and Boundaries Commission administers elections and, only after geography is established, public boundary processes; an Auditor-General audits and publishes public accounts; and an Integrity and Information Ombud investigates administrative complaints, oversees access-to-records duties and reports publicly. A merit-based Public Service Commission supervises public-service hiring and ethics. Members of these four bodies are selected through open public nomination and approval by three-fifths of the Assembly and a majority of the Council, serve one nonrenewable six-year term, report annually to both chambers and may be removed only for cause after independent judicial review. No real incumbents are named, and no institution is described as currently operational.

### Legal order and accountability

The fictional hierarchy is Constitution, Acts of the federal legislature, regulations made under a specific enabling Act, then executive instruments limited to implementation. New crimes, taxes and rights restrictions require legislation; rules take effect only after publication in a public register. Courts may invalidate unconstitutional action and provide remedies. Administrative decisions require reasons and a review route. Annual budgets, audited accounts, procurement awards, asset/conflict disclosures and oversight reports are public by default, subject to narrow, reasoned privacy and safety exceptions that can be independently reviewed.

### Citizenship and identity

BHF/004/2025’s established demonym remains **Bhoomians**. The new fictional Act provides citizenship by descent from either parent; by birth in future territory where parentage is unknown or the child would otherwise be stateless; and through naturalisation after five years’ lawful residence in territory later defined by canon. Applicants receive free civic orientation, but no language or knowledge exam is required. Decisions must be reasoned within 180 days and denials or delays may be appealed to an independent court. Discrimination by ethnicity, religion, sex, language or political opinion is prohibited. Dual nationality is allowed. Citizenship cannot be lost if that would make a person stateless; voluntary renunciation requires proof of another nationality, and deprivation for proven fraud requires a court hearing and appeal. A protected nationality register is a statutory design only: no records, application form or live application route are created in this slice.

## Emergency and amendment safeguards

An emergency may be declared only for armed attack, major natural disaster, severe public-health threat or grave constitutional danger. Reasons and measures must be published; the legislature must approve within seven days. The declaration lasts up to 30 days and may be renewed for 30-day periods only by two-thirds of each chamber. Courts and legislatures cannot be suspended, the Constitution cannot be amended during an emergency, and courts may review each measure. Continuation beyond 90 cumulative days requires approval by referendum and fresh judicial review every 30 days.

Ordinary constitutional amendments require two-thirds of each chamber and approval in a national referendum. Changes to the rights chapter or the federal balance also require ratification by two-thirds of member units once the unit schedule exists. Human dignity, equal citizenship, elected representation, independent courts and the federal character cannot be abolished by amendment. These higher thresholds protect continuity at the cost of making changes harder; the source research documents alternatives and their trade-offs.
