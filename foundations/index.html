New fictional canon · 3 November 2026

Constitutional foundations

A first, coherent framework for rights, government, public law and citizenship—authored as new fictional canon, not recovered from the 2025 archive.

Fictional framework only. The provisions on this page have no real-world legal effect and make no claim of real-world statehood or recognition. They do not describe an operating government, court, public office, citizenship registry or live application.

The original 2025 records remain unchanged. No territory, capital, boundary, population, or member-unit names are supplied; the member-unit schedule is intentionally blank.

1. Constitution

BHF/006/2026 · New fictional canon

The Constitution is the highest rule in this fictional legal order. It protects every person, allocates only the powers it names to the federation, and leaves a future sphere of self-government for member units whose names and number are not yet established.

The 2025 Founding Proclamation states four founding values. The 2026 Constitution carries those values forward without changing the historical record.

  • Clarity of thought
  • Ethical governance
  • Dignity of individual existence
  • Collective peace

Federal structure without invented geography

The charter describes a civic federation but does not supply its territory, capital, boundaries, member-unit count or unit names. The member-unit schedule remains blank. The federation’s enumerated responsibilities are a common rights floor, citizenship, national elections and constitutional adjudication. Local self-government belongs to member units only after later canon lawfully defines them. Shared policy areas require legislation that states who is responsible and how governments coordinate; federal law prevails only where the Constitution expressly grants that priority. Inter-unit disputes are for the Constitutional Court.

The proposed People’s Council has one delegate and one equal vote for each future member unit. Each unit selects its delegate under its own published constitution for a four-year term. Until a member-unit schedule exists, the Council and all Council-dependent appointments remain part of the framework, not operating institutions.

Rights, limits and remedies

Rights belong to every person, not only citizens. The protected floor includes equality, dignity, life, conscience, expression, peaceful assembly, association, privacy, movement, fair hearing, access to courts, an effective remedy and access to public information. Children receive explicit rights to identity, nationality safeguards and a meaningful chance to express their views. Any restriction must be in a published general law, pursue a legitimate public purpose, be necessary and proportionate, use no broader measure than needed, and leave the right’s core intact.

Courts may review government action, invalidate unconstitutional rules and order remedies. During an emergency, life, dignity, freedom from torture and slavery, legal personality, protection from retroactive criminal punishment, freedom of conscience, court access and review of detention cannot be suspended.

Emergency and amendment safeguards

An emergency may be declared only for armed attack, major natural disaster, severe public-health threat or grave constitutional danger. The declaration, reasons and measures must be published, and the Assembly must approve it within seven days. A declaration lasts at most 30 days; renewal for another 30 days requires a two-thirds vote in each chamber. Courts and the Assembly cannot be suspended, courts may review every measure, and the Constitution cannot be amended during an emergency. Continuation beyond 90 cumulative days also requires a referendum and fresh judicial review every 30 days.

Ordinary amendments require two-thirds of each chamber and approval in a national referendum. An amendment to the rights chapter or federal balance also needs ratification by two-thirds of the member units once that schedule exists. Human dignity, equal citizenship, elected representation, independent courts and the federal character cannot be abolished. Higher thresholds protect continuity but make change slower and harder.

2. Government Directory

BHF/007/2026 · New fictional canon

This directory explains the designed institutions and their checks. It does not claim that elections have occurred or that any body is operating.

The Chief Architect · Head of Country

The Chief Architect is the founder and current Head of Country in this fictional canon. This is a founder-specific, non-elective office: the position is not elected or appointed. The founder is identified only by the title Chief Architect. No personal name is supplied. No end date is stated. While the office is held, its limited duties are ceremonial and constitutional continuity. The Chief Architect does not direct the Cabinet, control the Assembly or decide cases.

After the founder’s tenure ends, the office remains vacant permanently. It cannot be filled by election or appointment, and there is no successor Head of Country. The design creates no replacement officeholder or rotating substitute.

The elected People’s Assembly

The People’s Assembly is the elected legislature, chosen every four years by proportional representation. The Constitution requires elections on that cycle; this archive does not claim an election has taken place. The Assembly passes federal laws and budgets, oversees the executive and public administration, and holds hearings and inquiries in public.

After the Chief Architect’s office becomes vacant, the Assembly becomes the nation’s collective supervisory authority. The authority belongs to the Assembly as a body and is exercised through resolutions, hearings, inquiries, budget scrutiny and confidence procedures. The Assembly is not a successor Head of Country; neither the Assembly nor its Speaker inherits that office. It cannot direct courts or override independent institutions.

People’s Council, Prime Minister and Cabinet

The People’s Council represents future member units on an equal basis: one delegate and one vote for each. Its membership and selection procedures cannot operate until future canon defines the units. The two chambers jointly pass federal laws and budgets.

The Prime Minister is chosen by the Assembly and remains in office only while it has confidence. A constructive no-confidence vote must name a successor Prime Minister, avoiding a leadership vacuum. The Cabinet is accountable to the Assembly. Ministries can be created by statute and budget rather than listed in advance without a defined need.

Independent courts and appointments

Courts are independent. Ordinary judges are selected through open, merit-based competition on a Judicial Appointments Commission recommendation. They serve until resignation, the fixed retirement age of 70, or removal for proven incapacity or serious misconduct after independent tribunal review. While the Chief Architect’s office is occupied, the Chief Architect formally appoints judges on the Commission’s recommendation. After the office becomes vacant, the Commission completes ordinary appointments from its public shortlist.

The nine-member Constitutional Court has staggered, nonrenewable 12-year terms. A public shortlist must receive a three-fifths Assembly vote and a majority of the Council. While the Chief Architect’s office is occupied, the Chief Architect then makes the formal appointment; after the office becomes vacant, the appointment takes effect when both chamber votes are complete—no successor signature is needed.

The Judicial Appointments Commission has nine members: three judges elected by their peers, two members selected by the legal profession, two public members chosen by the Assembly (one from the governing majority and one from the opposition), and two unit delegates chosen by the Council. Members serve one nonrenewable five-year term. The Commission cannot operate until the Council exists. Constitutional Court judges may be removed only for proven incapacity or serious misconduct after independent tribunal review and a two-thirds vote in both chambers.

Independent public institutions

Electoral and Boundaries Commission
Administers elections and, only after geography is established, public boundary processes.
Auditor-General
Audits and publishes public accounts, budgets and financial management.
Integrity and Information Ombud
Investigates administrative complaints, oversees access-to-records duties and reports findings publicly.
Public Service Commission
Oversees merit-based public-service hiring and ethics standards.

Members of these four bodies are nominated publicly and approved by a three-fifths vote in the Assembly and a majority in the Council. They serve one nonrenewable six-year term, report annually to both chambers and may be removed only for cause after independent judicial review. Council-dependent appointments remain in the future framework until member units are defined; no incumbents or live institutions are claimed.

3. Legal Order

BHF/008/2026 · New fictional canon

The fictional hierarchy is: Constitution; Acts passed by the federal legislature; regulations made under a specific enabling Act; and executive instruments that only implement that Act. No official can create a crime, tax or restriction of rights by executive instrument alone. Rules take effect only after publication in a public register; a Chief Architect’s signature is not required.

Administrative decisions must give reasons, follow fair procedures and offer a route to independent review. Courts may invalidate unconstitutional action and provide an effective remedy. Public budgets, audited accounts, procurement awards, asset and conflict disclosures, and oversight reports are public by default. Any privacy or safety exception must be narrow, explained and open to independent review.

These are legal-design rules, not a claim that a register, court or public administration is operating. Comparative constitutional texts help frame the questions, but no foreign law is copied into Bhoomi canon.

4. Citizenship and Identity

BHF/009/2026 · New fictional canon

The original 2025 record names the demonym Bhoomians but leaves eligibility and a grant process unstated. The following rules are new 2026 fictional canon, not a retroactive reading of that record.

  • Citizenship may pass by descent from either parent.
  • Once territory is defined by later canon, a child born there whose parentage is unknown or who would otherwise be stateless receives a route to citizenship.
  • Naturalisation is available after five years of lawful residence in territory later defined by canon.
  • Applicants receive free civic orientation; no language or knowledge exam is required. Decisions must be reasoned within 180 days. A denial or delay may be appealed to an independent court.
  • Discrimination based on ethnicity, religion, sex, language or political opinion is prohibited. Dual nationality is allowed.
  • Citizenship cannot be lost if that would make a person stateless. Voluntary renunciation requires proof of another nationality; deprivation for proven fraud requires a court hearing and appeal.

A protected nationality register is a future legal design only. There are no people’s records or public application form, and there is no live application. Children’s identity, nationality safeguards and participation are informed by treaty texts as comparative references; those treaties are not represented as Bhoomi obligations.

Design choices and trade-offs

Comparative constitutions do not agree on one federal map, one way to limit rights, or one amendment threshold. This draft assigns only defined national powers and leaves unit details open; that protects local choice but makes the Council and several appointments dependent on future member units. A strict rights floor and strong emergency review reduce executive abuse, while high amendment thresholds make needed changes harder. Independent appointments and public reporting constrain capture but can slow staffing, especially while the Council is undefined.

Canada’s time-limited override for specified rights differs from non-derogable-rights approaches in international human-rights law. This fictional charter instead requires proportionality and preserves a non-derogable core. That is a design choice, not a claim that one foreign model is best.
