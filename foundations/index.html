Constitution & Government

The Constitution and laws of The Bhoomi Federation establish the rights of every person, the institutions of government, public law and citizenship.

The Constitution sets out the Federation’s national powers and provides for self-government by member units. The member-unit schedule is to be established by law.

1. Constitution

BHF/006/2026 · 3 November 2026

The Constitution protects every person, allocates the powers of the Federation, and guarantees self-government to its member units.

The founding values established by the Founding Proclamation are clarity of thought, ethical governance, dignity of individual existence, and collective peace.

  • Clarity of thought
  • Ethical governance
  • Dignity of individual existence
  • Collective peace

BHF/003/2025 · Founding Proclamation

Federal structure

The Federation is responsible for the common rights floor, citizenship, national elections and constitutional adjudication. Member units exercise local self-government. Shared policy areas require legislation that defines responsibility and coordination; federal law prevails only where the Constitution expressly grants that priority. Inter-unit disputes are decided by the Constitutional Court.

The People’s Council represents member units on an equal basis, with one delegate and one vote for each. Each unit selects its delegate under its own published constitution for a four-year term. The Council convenes once the member-unit schedule is established; appointments that depend on the Council follow the same schedule.

Rights, limits and remedies

Rights belong to every person, not only citizens. They include equality, dignity, life, conscience, expression, peaceful assembly, association, privacy, movement, fair hearing, access to courts, an effective remedy and access to public information. Children have rights to identity, nationality safeguards and a meaningful opportunity to express their views. A restriction must be established by published general law, pursue a legitimate public purpose, be necessary and proportionate, use no broader measure than needed, and leave the core of the right intact.

Courts may review government action, invalidate unconstitutional rules and order remedies. During an emergency, life, dignity, freedom from torture and slavery, legal personality, protection from retroactive criminal punishment, freedom of conscience, court access and review of detention cannot be suspended.

Emergency and amendment safeguards

An emergency may be declared only for armed attack, major natural disaster, severe public-health threat or grave constitutional danger. The declaration, reasons and measures must be published, and the Assembly must approve it within seven days. A declaration lasts at most 30 days; renewal for another 30 days requires a two-thirds vote in each chamber. Courts and the Assembly cannot be suspended, courts may review every measure, and the Constitution cannot be amended during an emergency. Continuation beyond 90 cumulative days requires a referendum and fresh judicial review every 30 days.

Ordinary amendments require two-thirds of each chamber and approval in a national referendum. Amendments to the rights chapter or federal balance also require ratification by two-thirds of the member units once their schedule is established. Human dignity, equal citizenship, elected representation, independent courts and the federal character of the Federation cannot be abolished.

2. Government and institutions

BHF/007/2026 · 3 November 2026

The Chief Architect · “First Person”

The founder alone may hold the Chief Architect position, also known as the “First Person.” During the founder’s tenure, the Chief Architect has supreme authority above the Constitution and all institutions.

After the founder’s tenure ends, the Chief Architect position is permanently vacant and cannot be held by anyone. The Assembly supervises the nation collectively under the Constitution; it does not succeed to the Chief Architect position or become a successor Head of Country.

The elected People’s Assembly

The People’s Assembly is the elected legislature, chosen every four years by proportional representation. It passes federal laws and budgets, oversees the executive and public administration, and holds public hearings and inquiries.

People’s Council, Prime Minister and Cabinet

The People’s Council represents member units on an equal basis: one delegate and one vote for each. The two chambers jointly pass federal laws and budgets.

The Prime Minister is chosen by the Assembly and remains in office only while it has confidence. A constructive no-confidence vote must name a successor Prime Minister. The Cabinet is accountable to the Assembly. Ministries are established by statute and budget. During the founder’s tenure, the Prime Minister, Cabinet, Assembly and all other institutions remain below the Chief Architect’s supreme authority.

Independent courts and appointments

Courts are independent. Ordinary judges are selected through open, merit-based competition on the recommendation of a Judicial Appointments Commission. They serve until resignation, the fixed retirement age of 70, or removal for proven incapacity or serious misconduct after independent tribunal review. While the Chief Architect’s office is occupied, the Chief Architect formally appoints judges on the Commission’s recommendation. After the office becomes vacant, the Commission completes ordinary appointments from its public shortlist.

The nine-member Constitutional Court has staggered, nonrenewable 12-year terms. A public shortlist must receive a three-fifths Assembly vote and a majority of the Council. While the Chief Architect’s office is occupied, the Chief Architect makes the formal appointment; after the office becomes vacant, the appointment takes effect when both chamber votes are complete.

The Judicial Appointments Commission has nine members: three judges elected by their peers, two members selected by the legal profession, two public members chosen by the Assembly (one from the governing majority and one from the opposition), and two unit delegates chosen by the Council. Members serve one nonrenewable five-year term. Constitutional Court judges may be removed only for proven incapacity or serious misconduct after independent tribunal review and a two-thirds vote in both chambers.

Independent public institutions

Electoral and Boundaries Commission
Administers elections and public boundary processes once the Federation’s geography is established.
Auditor-General
Audits and publishes public accounts, budgets and financial management.
Integrity and Information Ombud
Investigates administrative complaints, oversees access-to-records duties and reports findings publicly.
Public Service Commission
Oversees merit-based public-service hiring and ethics standards.

Members are nominated publicly and approved by a three-fifths vote in the Assembly and a majority in the Council. They serve one nonrenewable six-year term, report annually to both chambers and may be removed only for cause after independent judicial review.

3. Public law and administration

BHF/008/2026 · 3 November 2026

The legal order is: the Constitution; Acts passed by the federal legislature; regulations made under a specific enabling Act; and executive instruments that implement that Act. No official may create a crime, tax or restriction of rights by executive instrument alone. Rules take effect only after publication in the public register; a Chief Architect’s signature is not required.

Administrative decisions must give reasons, follow fair procedures and offer a route to independent review. Courts may invalidate unconstitutional action and provide an effective remedy. Public budgets, audited accounts, procurement awards, asset and conflict disclosures, and oversight reports are public by default. Any privacy or safety exception must be narrow, explained and open to independent review.

4. Citizenship and identity

BHF/009/2026 · 3 November 2026

Citizens are known as Bhoomians. BHF/004/2025 established the designation; the Citizenship and Identity Act, issued on 3 November 2026, sets out citizenship eligibility and procedure.

  • Citizenship may pass by descent from either parent.
  • A child born in the Federation whose parentage is unknown or who would otherwise be stateless receives a route to citizenship.
  • Naturalisation is available after five years of lawful residence in the Federation.
  • Applicants receive free civic orientation; no language or knowledge examination is required. Decisions must be reasoned within 180 days. A denial or delay may be appealed to an independent court.
  • Discrimination based on ethnicity, religion, sex, language or political opinion is prohibited. Dual nationality is allowed.
  • Citizenship cannot be lost if that would make a person stateless. Voluntary renunciation requires proof of another nationality; deprivation for proven fraud requires a court hearing and appeal.
